Every product starts as a choice about what not to build. There are more annoying tasks in a normal day than any company could take on, so the first job is deciding which one deserves our time. These are the questions we ask before we write any code.

## Is the task real?

We look for tasks people already do, not tasks we hope they will start doing. If someone has built a workaround, such as a spreadsheet, a notebook or a group chat used for something it was never meant for, the need is real. The workaround shows us what matters to them and where it breaks.

## Does it come round often?

A task that happens once a year can be painful, but a tool for it is easy to forget. We prefer problems people meet every day or every week. Small savings on a frequent task add up, and frequent use means we learn quickly whether we got it right.

## Would people be better off?

Some software saves time and then spends it on notifications, streaks and feeds. We do not want to build that. Before we commit, we ask what a person's day looks like once the product works. If the honest answer is "about the same, with one more app", we keep looking.

## Can we make it simpler, not just different?

Plenty of tasks already have software. That alone does not rule them out, but we need a clear idea of what we would remove. If we cannot name the steps that disappear, we have not found our product yet.

## What happens next

Once a problem passes these questions, we write down the smallest version that would genuinely help and build that first. You can see where that has led on our [products page](/products), and read how we [keep software simple as it grows](/blog/why-simple-software-is-harder-to-build).
